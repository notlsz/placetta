# Win markets in waves, not everywhere

No company in this evidence base has won every market, and an AI startup that tries to will lose money in most of them. The pattern across 40 years of cross-border battles is consistent: **locals win where they remove a structural friction the global player treats as secondary** (escrow for trust, cash for the unbanked, a native-language product before the giant localizes), and **globals win where they run locally and arrive early** (KFC China, Netflix's local originals, Google Pay on India's UPI rail). For AI specifically, the map is unusual: outside China and Russia a global product already dominates (ChatGPT holds about **75% in India and Germany and about 80% in the US**), so your real rival in most countries is not a local startup but OpenAI or Google, and your opening is to be the "local" against their generic product ([OfficeChai](https://officechai.com/ai/gemini-competitive-with-chatgpt-in-japan-and-south-korea-lags-in-us-and-india)). Attacking many markets at once works for **availability** (near-zero cost to ship a model everywhere) and fails for **capital** (Uber spent about $700M in Southeast Asia before selling to Grab) ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/04/11/uber-global-exits-billions/amp/)). Culture matters, but the evidence is strong only for language, payments and channels; deeper cultural redesign has thin, small-sample support, so treat it as a hypothesis to A/B test per market. The realistic target: lead in 2 to 4 beachheads, be present everywhere, enter Japan, Korea and the Gulf through partners, serve China only from outside, and stay out of Russia.

> Notation: **†** marks a claim the research notes flagged as unverified (prior knowledge, not re-checked). Treat † items as leads to confirm, not facts. Ipsos trust figures come from the [Ipsos AI Monitor 2025](https://www.ipsos.com/sites/default/files/ct/news/documents/2025-06/Ipsos-AI-Monitor-2025_0.pdf) (30 countries, 23,216 adults); the notes warn the PDF table alignment was imperfect, so check the charts before quoting.

## Thirty battles show locals win on friction, globals win by going local

The cases sort cleanly once you separate **product/behavior wins** from **structural wins** (regulation, censorship, capital, ecosystem). Most famous "local beats global" stories were decided on product before the state got involved.

## Locals win by removing friction, not by being more "cultural"

Your thesis that understanding culture, psychology, society and history lets a brand win is **half right**. The evidence ranks adaptation types very differently, and the cases that look cultural usually turn out to be structural on closer reading.

| Adaptation type | Evidence strength | Key finding |
|---|---|---|
| Native language | **Strong** (8,709 consumers, 29 countries) | 76% prefer buying with info in their language; 40% never buy from sites in other languages ([CSA Research](https://www.newswire.com/news/survey-of-8-709-consumers-in-29-countries-finds-that-76-prefer-21174283)). Stated preference, from a localization vendor |
| Product/workflow adaptation | **Strong** (meta-analyses of 17, 26 and 110 samples) | Product standardization hurts financial performance; promotion standardization helps; B2B tolerates more standardization than consumer ([Tan & Sousa 2013](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/manint/v53y2013i5d10.1007_s11575-013-0172-5.html); [Mandler et al. 2021](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/170999/1/Manuscript_Performance%20consequences%20of%20marketing%20standardizationadaptation.pdf)) |
| Payments, trust infrastructure, channels | **Strong (case-consistent)** | Alipay escrow, Careem cash, Zalando invoice, Grab lending, MELI payments; M-Pesa SA shows the reverse |
| Culture-driven UI/visual redesign | **Weak to modest** (N = 105; small lab studies) | Hofstede rules predicted preferred UI ~61% vs 33% chance, but at chance in Rwanda, and no conversion test ([Reinecke & Bernstein 2013](https://wildlab.cs.washington.edu/Publications_files/ReineckeBernstein_MIS-Q_2013.pdf)); congruent sites raised willingness to pay for Korean but not US consumers ([Ko, Seo & Jung 2015](https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/mktlet/v26y2015i3p377-390.html)) |
| National culture scores as predictors | **Modest** (598 studies, 200k+ people) | Mean individual-level effect ρ = 0.18; stronger in "tight" cultures ([Taras, Kirkman & Steel 2010](https://prism.ucalgary.ca/items/493650c4-2e30-4994-b2fd-38dc17686197/full)) |
| Culture and adoption timing | **Strong** (16 countries, 10 products) | New products take off ~6 years after launch on average; Scandinavia takes about half as long as the Mediterranean; culture explains takeoff better than economics ([Stremersch & Tellis 2004](https://personal.eur.nl/stremersch/artikels/Understanding%20and%20managing%20international%20growth%20of%20new%20products.pdf)) |
| "History/psychology insight causes wins" | **Untested** | No causal study found; plausible, folklore-grade |

Read the cases with that ladder. Walmart Germany is taught as a culture failure, but the contemporaneous analysis says it simply couldn't undercut Aldi and Lidl from a fragmented store base ([CBS](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/wal-marts-german-flop)). McDonald's Iceland was a currency collapse. WeChat in India died of product mechanics (friend model, default location sharing, app size) that a good local PM would have caught, not of a missing grasp of Indian history ([FactorDaily](https://archive.factordaily.com/how-wechat-faded-into-the-silence-in-india/)). Mixi had the *best* cultural fit (anonymity) and still lost once smartphones and the 2011 earthquake changed behavior ([Eurasia Review](https://www.eurasiareview.com/03072012-facebook-takes-off-in-japan-as-local-network-mixi-struggles/)). The genuinely behavioral wins (Home Depot's DIY mismatch, Zalando accepting buy-try-return on invoice ([The Local](https://www.thelocal.com/20120530/42843))) are about **who does the job and how people pay**, which you can observe directly rather than infer from Hofstede scores.

The academic frameworks agree. Khanna and Palepu's institutional voids ([HBR](https://hbr.org/2005/06/strategies-that-fit-emerging-markets)) and Bhattacharya and Michael's six local tactics ([HBR excerpt](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/how-local-companies-keep-multinationals-at-bay)) both describe locals filling missing infrastructure. Johanson and Vahlne's revised Uppsala model reframes the handicap as **outsidership**, not foreignness: you lose because you're outside the local business network ([JIBS](https://doi.org/10.1057/jibs.2009.24)). That is fixable with local co-founders, partners and reference customers faster than with cultural research. The verdict: **localize language, payments, channels and trust signals everywhere; treat deeper cultural redesign as a hypothesis and A/B test it market by market**, with the biggest expected payoff in high-uncertainty-avoidance markets (Japan, Korea, Germany, France).

## Sprinkler for availability, waterfall for money

**Yes, you can attack multiple markets at once, but only at the availability layer.** The evidence splits by what you're spreading.

| Factor | Favors simultaneous (sprinkler) | Favors sequential (waterfall) |
|---|---|---|
| Adaptation cost per market | Low (AI handles language) | High (compliance, integrations, sales teams) |
| Competitor lead time | Short; copying is fast | Long |
| Network effects | Cross-border (global network) | Local clusters (city, country) |
| Fixed entry cost | Low (digital launch) | High (entity, residency, local hires) |
| Capital | Abundant | Limited |

Sources for the conditions: Ayal and Zif ([Journal of Marketing 1979](https://doi.org/10.1177/002224297904300209)) and Kalish, Mahajan and Muller ([TAU record](https://cris.tau.ac.il/en/publications/waterfall-and-sprinkler-new-product-strategies-in-competitive-glo/)). The best empirical test, Stremersch and Tellis (10 consumer durables, 16 European countries, 1950 to 2000), found sprinkler produced **more sales** because market size outweighed small cross-country learning effects, but carried the **highest risk**; a north-to-south waterfall had the lowest ([PDF](https://personal.eur.nl/stremersch/artikels/Understanding%20and%20managing%20international%20growth%20of%20new%20products.pdf)). No rigorous study exists for apps, SaaS or AI, and a 2025 meta-analysis of 378 studies on early internationalization exists but its effect sizes for speed and scope were not extracted in this research ([Fariborzi et al.](https://metatoc.com/papers/202235-early-internationalization-a-meta-analysis-of-antecedents-dimensions-and-performance)); don't claim speed itself predicts success.

The case evidence fills the gap. Netflix switched on 130 countries in one day, then spent on originals and pricing only where traction appeared. Temu reached 49 countries in about 16 months and 90+ in 31, and met the backlash that scale invites: an EU DSA preliminary breach finding (Jul 2025), the end of US de minimis (May 2025), and blocks in Indonesia and Vietnam ([Temu mirror](https://cliffipedia.org/content/wikipedia_en_all_maxi_2025-08/Temu)). Uber's simultaneous subsidy war against city-cluster champions ended in exits everywhere. Zhu and Iansiti explain why: Uber's networks are isolated city clusters that rivals attack one at a time, while Airbnb's is one global cluster ([MAAW summary](https://maaw.info/ArticleSummaries/ArtSumZhuandIansiti2019.htm)).

AI products sit in an advantageous spot: weak local network effects, near-zero marginal cost per country, and usage that grows fastest in lower-income countries ([Digit on OpenAI/NBER](https://www.digit.in/?p=1154993)). But revenue follows income: **1% higher GDP per capita goes with 0.7% higher per-capita usage** ([Anthropic Economic Index](https://anthropic.com/research/economic-index-geography)), and only **18% of frequent work users pay for public AI tools** ([HR Online on KPMG](https://humanresourcesonline.net/new-global-study-reveals-high-public-adoption-of-ai-across-47-countries)). The answer is the hybrid your draft gestures at: **be usable in every legal market from day one, and concentrate sales, partnerships, local hires and paid acquisition on the 2 to 4 markets where retention and payment data prove traction**, then the next wave. This hybrid is an inference from the evidence, not a tested finding, so instrument it.

## For AI, the giant is global and regulation sets the order

AI breaks the usual "local vs global" frame. Outside China and Russia, a strong global product already beats local champions even in big non-English markets.

| Market | ChatGPT share (Jan 2026) | Who else matters |
|---|---|---|
| US | ~80% | Gemini, Claude, Copilot |
| India | ~75% | Gemini; Sarvam, Krutrim† |
| Germany | ~75% | Aleph Alpha pivoted, now being acquired by Cohere |
| UK | >70% | Gemini |
| Japan | ~57% | Gemini second |
| South Korea | ~48% | Gemini, Adot (SK Telecom), Alice |
| Russia | ~25% | GigaChat (Sber), YandexGPT |
| China | Minimal | Doubao 382M MAU, Qwen 167M, DeepSeek 130M (May 2026) |

Sources: [OfficeChai (Similarweb + Sensor Tower)](https://officechai.com/ai/gemini-competitive-with-chatgpt-in-japan-and-south-korea-lags-in-us-and-india); [TechNode/QuestMobile](https://technode.com/2026/07/14/questmobile-chinas-ai-native-apps-reach-499-million-monthly-active-users/).

Three implications follow. First, **local champions win only where a state or ecosystem supplies distribution** (Douyin to Doubao, Sber, Korean telcos). Model-only national champions struggle: Aleph Alpha abandoned frontier models in 2024, and Cohere agreed to acquire it in Apr 2026 with Schwarz Group (Lidl's parent) investing $600M, pitched as sovereign AI for regulated European sectors ([Reuters via WHBL](https://whbl.com/2026/04/24/canadas-ai-startup-cohere-buys-germanys-aleph-alpha-to-expand-in-europe/)). Don't compete on model IQ; compete on workflow ownership, where the generic assistant is eBay and you are Taobao.

Second, **trust is inverted**. Emerging economies trust AI far more (57% vs 39% in advanced economies) and accept it more (84% vs 65%), while trust overall fell from 63% to 56% between 2022 and 2024 ([KPMG/Melbourne](https://kpmg.com/kpmg-us/content/dam/kpmg/pdf/2025/trust-attitudes-artificial-intelligence-executive-summary.pdf)). UAE (91%) and Saudi (88%) lead AI use; Germany, the Netherlands and Canada sit below 50% trust ([People Matters ME](https://me.peoplemattersglobal.com/news/technology/gulf-nations-lead-in-ai-trust-adoption-and-digital-literacy-heres-what-the-data-tells-us-45405)). Lead with capability and access in India, Indonesia, Mexico, Nigeria and the Gulf; lead with assurance (audit trails, residency, human review) in the US, UK, France and Germany.

Third, **language quality is a unit-cost problem, not just a UX one**. On AfriMMLU, 16 African languages trail English by about 25 points; each extra token per word costs up to 8 to 18 accuracy points, and doubling token counts doubles inference cost and latency ([arXiv 2509.05486](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05486v1)). Measure accuracy and cost per task in each target language before you commit sales money there.

## Your draft holds up; sharpen five parts and drop the numbers

Your framework (standardize the global AI core, localize surface and constraints, concentrate on a few beachheads) matches the evidence better than most published playbooks. Here is what to keep, fix and add.

| Draft element | Verdict | What the evidence says |
|---|---|---|
| Parallel selection, sequenced conquest | **Keep** | Matches sprinkler/waterfall evidence and Uber's failures |
| Standardize core, localize surface | **Keep, sharpen** | Meta-analyses: adapt the *product* (standardizing it hurts financial performance); standardize the *brand message* (it helps). Localize workflows and integrations, not the brand promise |
| Unit-of-value table | **Keep, fix sources** | Verified: Alipay escrow, Wang Wang chat, Careem cash, WeChat red envelopes, Yum China membership and local operator. Unverified in the research: Taobao "bargaining", Flipkart cash on delivery, Grab cash/bikes, KFC menu items† |
| Choose by friction, not GDP | **Partly right** | Friction predicts adoption; GDP predicts revenue (0.7 elasticity). Score both, plus regulatory gate, network shape and **who already owns distribution** (VK, TikTok India) |
| Three tiers (beachhead, fast-follow, platform) | **Keep** | The best available hybrid; untested for AI, so instrument it |
| "Market #8 costs 30 to 50% of market #1" | **Unsourced** | No evidence found. Treat as a target you measure, not a planning assumption |
| Onboarding <10 minutes | **Unsourced target** | Reasonable design goal; no study supports the specific number |
| Localized trust messaging | **Keep, strengthen** | KPMG and Ipsos confirm the advanced/emerging trust split |
| Brand last | **Keep** | Meituan refused the 2011 ad war and survived 5,000 rivals |
| Regional pods own revenue | **Keep** | eBay's San Jose override is the canonical counterexample; KFC's local operator the proof |
| Anti-playbook | **Keep, add five** | Add: subsidy-fighting a local network champion (Uber); assuming the friction exists (M-Pesa SA); inheriting bad assets (Walmart Germany, Target Canada); single-country geopolitical exposure (TikTok India); HQ veto on local product decisions (eBay) |
| Missing | **Add** | Exit and partner options (merge for equity, sell to the giant, buy the local); token-tax unit economics; regulation-driven entry order; time-to-local-version as a KPI |

### The sharpened playbook

| Step | Move | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Map the friction | For each candidate market, name the one structural friction (payment, trust, language, device, regulation) a generic assistant ignores, and confirm it exists with 20 design partners | M-Pesa SA vs Kenya; Home Depot China |
| 2. Score markets on two axes | Adoption pull (friction, AI excitement, token cost) and revenue pull (GDP per capita, WTP, procurement); then filter by regulatory gate and distribution owner | Anthropic Economic Index; KPMG; regulation table |
| 3. Ship everywhere, spend in waves | Self-serve availability in all legal markets; sales, partners and local hires only in 2 to 4 beachheads; graduate a market when retention and paid conversion clear a bar you set in advance | Netflix; Stremersch & Tellis |
| 4. Become the insider | Local co-founder or GM with P&L authority, local partners, named reference customers; HQ owns model, safety, eval and price architecture only | Johanson & Vahlne; eBay vs KFC |
| 5. Own one workflow end to end | 10x on one job in the local system (local documents, taxes, messaging app, payment rail), measured by task completion and cost per resolved task | Taobao escrow; MELI payments; Naver AI Briefing |
| 6. Outlast, don't outspend | No brand ads before retention; let the giant fight on many fronts; keep cash | Meituan; Grab |
| 7. Pre-decide the exit options | Where a local owns distribution, partner, license or merge for equity rather than fight | Uber's $12.5B in stakes; eBay's 19.5% of MELI; Careem |

## Market verdicts: where to attack, partner or stay out

| Verdict | Markets | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Attack directly (beachhead candidates)** | US, UK, India, Brazil, Mexico, Indonesia | Open regulation, global products already accepted, WhatsApp or self-serve channels; US/UK for revenue, India/Brazil/Mexico/Indonesia for volume and AI enthusiasm |
| **Attack with compliance build** | Germany, France, Italy, Spain | High WTP but skeptical; EU AI Act and residency are entry tickets; partner with SIs or sovereignty players rather than fight Mistral or SAP |
| **Enter through partners** | Japan, South Korea, Gulf, Turkey, Thailand, Vietnam, Philippines, Nigeria, Kenya | Distribution owned by SIers (Japan), Kakao/Naver/telcos (Korea), sovereign buyers (Gulf), super-apps and wallets (SEA, Africa) |
| **Serve only from outside / license** | China | CAC pre-approval, near-free domestic models, Airbnb and Uber precedent; serve Chinese companies expanding abroad instead |
| **Do not enter** | Russia | US/EU software and cloud sanctions (2024); Yandex and Sber own distribution |
| **Partner with heavy diligence** | Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan | Accessible but sanctions-transshipment risk; Kaspi-type super-apps own the customer |

Pick beachheads by your workflow, not this list's order. A US revenue anchor plus one high-volume WhatsApp market (Brazil or India) plus one EU gateway (UK or Germany) is the default mix the evidence supports.

## Conclusion

The question "can I win every market?" hides a better one: **which friction can I own that the global assistant will treat as secondary, and in which countries does that friction exist and pay?** The historical winners weren't cultural anthropologists; they were operators who noticed that strangers needed escrow, riders needed cash, Germans wanted to pay after delivery and Kenyans had no bank, then built for that before anyone else did. For an AI company the same logic points at workflow depth inside local systems (documents, tax rules, messaging apps, payment rails), which a horizontal model provider won't prioritize country by country. That is where your "localize deeply" instinct pays off, and also where it should be disciplined by measurement rather than theory.

The second shift is that **AI turns regulation and token economics into strategy**. Entry order is now set by CAC filings, the EU's 2026 to 2028 deadlines and Korea's representative thresholds as much as by market size, and serving a language profitably depends on its tokenizer as much as its population. Build the compliance and evaluation kit once, run each market as a measured experiment, and keep the exit options (partner, license, merge for equity) on the table from day one. Uber lost three continents and still banked $12.5B in stakes; knowing which fights to decline is part of winning.
