# Placetta growth playbook: winning the top 100 markets

Last updated 2026-11-01. Shared copy: https://claude.ai/artifact/CzMFG1aHeh6qSHxc3RDEnb. Evidence: `research/`.

## 1. The answer

Placetta wins country by country the way Ctrip and Taobao won China: by owning the one friction the global players treat
as secondary. Ours is bureaucracy. In every country the rival is not OpenAI or a global corporate-services firm; it is
the local profession businesses hire to stand in line for them: the gestoría in Spain, the despachante in Brazil, the
行政書士 in Japan, the 代办 agency in China, the PRO and typing centre in the Gulf, the permit expediter in the US.

Those incumbents share four weaknesses: one city, paper and phone, priced per job, and no memory once the store opens.
Placetta beats them with one agent that maps every license, fills the government's own forms in the local language,
files, follows up every day and keeps the store current, with a licensed local partner signing wherever the law
reserves the signature.

1. **Follow the customer, not the map.** Win corridors, not countries: the brands already crossing a border. China,
   Korea, Japan and Taiwan into the US first, then the same brands out to Canada, Mexico, the UK and the Gulf. One
   franchise brand opening in 10 countries is 10 markets on one contract.
2. **Be available everywhere, spend in waves.** The free license map works in any country from day one at near-zero
   cost; sales, partners and local hires go only to 2 to 4 markets at a time, graduated on retention and paid
   conversion (Netflix's 130 countries in a day, then money where traction showed).
3. **Become the insider, not the foreigner.** A licensed local partner, a local operator with authority and named local
   references beat any amount of cultural research (revised Uppsala model: you lose to outsidership, not foreignness).
4. **Outlast, don't outspend.** No subsidy wars, no brand ads before retention; where a local owns distribution,
   partner or buy instead of fighting (Meituan refused the 2011 ad war; Uber banked about $12.5B in stakes from markets
   it couldn't win).

"Dominate the top 100" means: available in all 100 in 2027, leading in 4 corridors within 12 months, and the default in a
market only once it holds the most openings filed there.

What the evidence says about culture (research/win-markets-evidence.md): language, payments, channels and trust signals
are strongly supported; deeper cultural redesign is weakly supported. Localize the first four everywhere; A/B test the
rest per market.

## 2. Scoring the 100 markets

A market earns money only where opening is painful, enough businesses are opening, and the law lets software do most of
the work. Score out of 100:

| Factor | Weight | Measured by | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bureaucracy pain | 25 | Procedures, days and cost to open a restaurant or shop, all permits | World Bank B-READY; our registry |
| Openings a year | 20 | New establishments, foreign-owned openings, greenfield projects | National registers; UNCTAD WIR |
| Corridor pull | 20 | How many existing customers' brands want to open there next | Our plan previews and asks, monthly |
| Who may file | 15 | Whether a non-licensed party may prepare and submit | Each country's practice law |
| Digital rails | 10 | One-stop online portals for company and license filings | Government portals; B-READY |
| Incumbent weakness | 10 | Price, speed and coverage of local intermediaries | Fee schedules; mystery-shop 5 |

Vetoes: sanctions or export controls (Russia, Iran, North Korea, Syria, Belarus: out), and any market needing a licence
we can't hold or partner for within 6 months (partner-only until we can). Don't rank by GDP alone: GDP predicts ability
to pay, not need.

## 3. The waves

See MARKETS.md for every market's wave. Rules:

- Wave 1 (beachheads, 2 to 4 at a time): full investment, local operator, signed partners, paid acquisition.
- Wave 2 (fast-follow): registry and partners built, sales through referrers only.
- Wave 3 (partner-only): enter through a licensed local firm or platform that owns distribution.
- Wave 4 (availability): the free license map and inbound requests only; no spend.
- Out: sanctioned or legally closed.

A market moves up only on the graduate bars below, and down after two quarters under the stop bars.

## 4. The operating system (market-in-a-box)

| Part | Built per country | Done when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| License registry | Every agency, license, form, fee and order for the top 5 business types in the top 5 cities | Odelia maps a restaurant and a shop with no human edits |
| Form library | The government's own forms, filled in the local language | 20 forms filled and checked by the local partner |
| Filing rail | Portal filing where a third party may file; a licensed partner's signature where required | First 10 filings accepted first try |
| Channel and payment | Updates in the owner's app (WeChat, WhatsApp, LINE, KakaoTalk); fees on the local rail, charged when approved | 80% of updates read there |
| Trust | A named bilingual specialist, a response time, in-region data where asked | 3 named local references |

Partners, signed before a country is marketed:

- **Licensed signers** (notary, 行政書士, despachante, chartered accountant): Odelia prepares, they review and sign for a
  per-filing fee. They become a channel, not a rival.
- **Counter runners**: someone at an agency counter or inspection within a day, paid per visit.
- **Referrers**: franchisors, landlords and brokers, banks, investment promotion agencies (SelectUSA, JETRO, KOTRA,
  InvestHK).

## 5. Metrics and kill rules

| Metric | Graduate | Stop after 2 quarters below |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plans previewed a month | 300 | 50 |
| Plan to paid | 8% | 2% |
| Filings approved first try | 85% | 60% |
| Days from filing to approval vs local incumbent | 2x faster | No faster |
| Net revenue retention | 110% | 90% |

Starting targets, not findings: measure, then adjust.

## 6. Anti-playbook (from the cases in research/)

- Subsidy-fighting a local network champion (Uber in China and Southeast Asia).
- Assuming the friction exists before 20 local businesses confirm it (M-Pesa South Africa).
- Opening too many markets before operations work (Target Canada).
- HQ overriding the local operator on product (eBay China).
- Claiming numbers we can't prove (the website's proof rule applies to every market page).
