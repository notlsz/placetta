## The claim

The landing page says "Licenses and permits in 92 cities worldwide" and the about page "92 cities in 22 countries, 12 kinds of business and 481 official forms". The guides data (site/guides/data.json) lists 92 cities in 22 countries, so the number matches what the guides publish. It does not mean every city is fully covered: below, 73 of the 92 have a verified filing for every core step, 14 are partial and 5 rest mostly on unverified rows. The registry now holds 626 entries (499 with a proof read from the official page, 127 unverified), up from 481. The landing copy was not changed.

Each row comes from what the registry returns for a new restaurant (staff, a build-out, a sign, a new company, alcohol) and a new shop at the city. A cell is `n✓ m?`: n filings with a proof (an official quote, URL and read date), m without (UNVERIFIED: the plan row abstains and names the office). `·` means nothing in the registry at that level for that topic. Topics: company, tax, employer, food, building (plan review, occupancy, zoning, signs, fire), licence (local business licence or registration), alcohol.

Status: **covered** when the city has a verified filing for a national company step, a tax registration, an employer registration, a food permit, a building or use step and a local licence; **partial** when at least three of those are verified; **unverified** otherwise. Gaps name the missing step.

## How the US checklist was built

From SBA's federal license table (https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/launch-your-business/apply-licenses-permits, which now lands on https://www.sba.gov/counseling/launch-your-business/#licenses-permits: agriculture imports with USDA, alcohol with TTB, fish and wildlife with FWS, radio with the FCC, transportation with DOT) and USAGov's small business pages (https://www.usa.gov/small-business, including its import license guide; the "america.gov" the founder named is retired). Then each state's own .gov page for formation, out-of-state registration, sales tax, employer accounts and workers' compensation, and each county's and city's own .gov pages. Secondary sources (law firms, blogs, aggregators) were used only to find leads; none is cited, and an entry whose official page couldn't be read is UNVERIFIED (an internal `lead` field may keep the secondary link for staff; it is never shown).
