What a furniture brand files to sell in a new market.

From the first container to the hundredth showroom, every filing comes from the agency's own rules. Odelia maps them for your markets, files with your approval and renews them before they lapse.

Importing into the US

  • Lacey Act declarationA plant and plant product declaration (PPQ Form 505) for wood products, filed by the importer. USDA APHIS
  • Formaldehyde rules for composite woodTSCA import certification, and a TSCA Title VI compliance label on finished goods with the fabricator's name and production date. EPA
  • Tip-over standardDressers, chests and armoires must pass three stability tests, ship with an anti-tip device and carry a certificate of compliance (16 CFR part 1261). CPSC

Selling in each state

  • California furniture licenseA state license before you operate as a retailer, importer, wholesaler or manufacturer of upholstered furniture. Bureau of Household Goods and Services
  • Sales tax and business registrationRegistration in each state where you open, sell or store inventory, with returns and renewals on a calendar.

Showrooms and warehouses

  • Every showroomBusiness license, certificate of occupancy and sign permits, in the order the city issues them.
  • Every warehouseFire and storage permits, and the inspections that keep them current.

Expanding overseas

  • EU Deforestation RegulationDue diligence for wood products placed on the EU market, applying to large companies from 30 December 2026. EY
  • EU General Product Safety RegulationProducts from outside the EU need a responsible economic operator in the EU. EUR-Lex
